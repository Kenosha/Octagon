What the measurements do—and do not—tell us

All 14 proper subsets of the original four families draw from the opening. All four families are therefore necessary for the original forced win. Individual targets can still be redundant, and a drawn game can have sharp tactics.

Distances assume that the winner wins as quickly as possible and the loser delays as long as possible. The replay fixes one lexicographically selected optimal line; it is not the complete strategy. A count of unique winning choices describes that line only. It does not measure how hard those moves are for a person to recognize.

The solver enumerates the complete legal state universe, reuses a movement graph without stopping at targets, and solves each target set separately. It then recomputes reachability with immediate wins and audits the outcome and distance equations at every reachable nonterminal state. Only the four valid quarter-turn rotations are used.

The original result matches the shipped tablebase in all aggregate counts and 2,048 separately sampled positions. Every decisive line is replayed by an independent JavaScript verifier. Infinite play and stalemate count as draws; there is no move-count limit. Fresh-game winning strategies strictly decrease distance and cannot repeat a position.
